How Humanity Labs works

Regulators and scientists regularly find medicines and food that are contaminated, mislabelled, sub-standard or fake. Their findings are scattered across websites, PDFs and press releases, and rarely reach the people holding the product. Humanity Labs gathers them, links every one to its source, and makes them easy to check.

Medicines: Batchlight

Batchlight reads medicine and medical device alerts published from January 2024 onward by the US FDA, the UK MHRA, Health Canada and the WHO. Our team also adds a few Indian alerts by hand, and each one says where it comes from. India's CDSCO monthly lists of failed medicine samples and state drug controllers' notices are not included yet. Batch numbers are extracted from each alert when it publishes them in a form we can read, so anyone can check the batch printed on their strip or box. Pharmacies, clinics and distributors can list their stock and be told when one of these sources flags a batch they hold. A batch number alone is never enough for a match: the product name, manufacturer or product code must agree too, and every match shows its reasons.

Food

The Food Safety module collects food recalls from the US FDA and the Canadian Food Inspection Agency automatically. India's FSSAI does not publish recalls in a form we can collect automatically, so our team adds a few Indian food safety actions by hand, with links; each one says whether its source is an official notice, an official lab result or a news report. You can search recalls by product, brand, batch code or barcode; read what official tests, peer-reviewed studies and news reports found in everyday foods; check a pack's FSSAI number, barcode and label; report a suspicious product; and find out how to get food tested properly.

Our evidence rules

  • Every alert and finding links to its source and is labelled: official source, official lab result, peer-reviewed study, news or NGO report, or provider's own information. Alerts added by our team are labelled the same way.
  • We never say a product is adulterated unless a regulator or an accredited laboratory has found it.
  • We don't offer home tests, photo checks or AI that claims to detect adulteration, because none has been shown to work reliably.
  • Reports from the public are reviewed by a curator and never published as findings.

What we are not

We are not a regulator or a laboratory, and this is not medical advice. "No alert found" does not prove that a product is genuine or safe. If an alert's notice says not to use a product, follow it, and see a doctor if someone has taken it. Otherwise, don't stop a medicine you need without asking a pharmacist or doctor.

Privacy

We store products and batch numbers, not patients. Food reports ask only for what is needed to follow up, and your connection's address is kept only as a one-way code to limit spam. We never sell data.
