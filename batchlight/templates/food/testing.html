Get food tested

Only a properly equipped laboratory can confirm what is in food. Here is how to choose an accredited lab, what to ask for, and what a test can cost.

How to get a sample tested in India

  1. Choose an accredited lab. Choose a lab accredited by NABL (the National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories) for the exact test you need, and preferably one notified (approved) by FSSAI for food testing. On NABL's website, check that your test is in the lab's accredited scope and that its certificate is valid.
  2. Ask for a specific test. "Is it adulterated?" can't be tested. Ask for named tests: for example pesticide residues, lead, aflatoxin, Salmonella, or a named adulterant.
  3. Keep the sample sealed. Use an unopened pack where possible, and note the batch number, barcode and where you bought it.
  4. Read the report against the legal limit. A result is only meaningful compared with the limit for that food.

Under Section 40 of the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, anyone who buys food can pay a fee and have it analysed by a Food Analyst appointed by the state's Commissioner of Food Safety. You must tell the seller when you buy the food that you plan to have it analysed. If the report shows the food does not comply with the law, your fee is refunded and the report goes to the Designated Officer for action. A report from a private lab is not a Section 40 report. Ask your state food safety department how to do this.
