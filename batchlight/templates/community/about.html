Four separate things

Each post shows four things, kept apart on purpose. None of them changes another.

Image assessment
What open-source vision models see in the photo, turned into a visible-condition score with its criteria and confidence.
Community verification
Community Notes add context. Their status comes from ratings across perspectives. Notes never change a score.
Scores and rankings
Averages of image assessments, one category at a time. Categories are never combined.
Discussion
Threaded comments. Votes on comments only order the discussion.

Official project records (costs, agencies, contractors) come from Infrastructure Transparency, are marked Official record with their source, and are never mixed with what contributors observe.

What a score is not. A score describes only what is visible in one photo at one moment. It is not a safety rating, an engineering inspection or a judgment about hidden or structural defects, and no part of Community claims that a structure is safe or unsafe.

Photos and privacy

  • JPEG, PNG or WebP, at most 12 MB, at least 320 pixels on the shorter side. Animated images are refused.
  • All metadata is removed: the photo is rotated as the camera intended, then saved again without EXIF, GPS or camera details. A position found in the photo is offered to you as the post's location; it is never kept in the image.
  • Photos that are too blurred or too dark to show anything are refused. Photos that are low-resolution, blurred, dark, washed out or flat are kept but not scored, and the reason is shown.
  • Before a photo is published, the detector looks for {{ PRIVACY_QUERIES|join(" and ") }} with a deliberately low threshold ({{ PRIVACY_THRESHOLD|pct }}) and blurs every match. Blurring something harmless costs little; missing a face would not.

Duplicate photos

A photo already shared is refused, and the earlier post is linked instead, so the same evidence is not counted twice.

  • Exact copies: the same file (SHA-256 of the upload), or the same pixels saved again in another format (SHA-256 of the decoded image).
  • Near copies: resized, recompressed or lightly edited versions. Two 64-bit fingerprints are compared, a perceptual hash and a difference hash; a photo counts as a copy only when both are close: at most {{ PHASH_MAX }} and {{ DHASH_MAX }} differing bits.
  • Repeats by the same person: a crop or a second, very similar photo of the same spot gets past both fingerprints. When the same contributor shares a photo of the same category within {{ REPEAT_METRES }} m and {{ REPEAT_HOURS }} hours of an earlier one, and the two are at least {{ REPEAT_SIMILARITY|pct }} alike to the image model, it stays visible but counts once in rankings. Similarity alone is not used across different people: different photos of similar potholes can look just as alike.

Scores

A score starts at 100. Each criterion removes up to its maximum points, and every post lists the points removed with the evidence and the method. The criteria for each category use descriptions of that category only.

  1. Visible condition. CLIP compares the photo with three descriptions of the category: in good, fair and poor condition. Points removed = maximum × (poor + 0.4 × fair).
  2. Damage-like regions. CLIPSeg outlines pixels whose probability of matching the category's damage descriptions is over {{ SEG_THRESHOLD|pct }}. The points grow with the outlined share of the photo and reach the maximum at {{ AREA_FULL|pct }}.
  3. Possible defects. OWLv2 marks boxes around the category's listed defects when its confidence is at least {{ DETECT_THRESHOLD|pct }}. The boxes are shown to help people look, but carry no points (see below).

Confidence

0.35 × photo quality (1 if usable, 0.5 if not) + 0.35 × category fit + 0.30 × agreement between the condition rating and the outline. High is 0.75 or more, medium 0.5 or more, otherwise low. Category fit is how likely the photo shows the chosen category compared with the most likely one.

When a photo is not scored

  • the photo is not clear enough (the problems are listed on the post), or
  • the category fit is under {{ NOT_SCORED_FIT|pct }}: the photo may not show the chosen kind of infrastructure.

An unscored post is still published and can be discussed and noted; it is left out of averages and rankings.

Rankings

  • Each category is ranked on its own, at five levels: {% for l in R.LEVELS %}{{ R.LEVEL_LABELS[l]|lower }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}. There is no combined ranking across categories.
  • A place's figure is the average score of its scored photos in that category, with the number of photos, the number of contributors, a 95% margin of error and the average confidence.
  • A place is ranked only with at least {{ R.MIN_RANK }} scored photos from at least {{ R.MIN_CONTRIBUTORS }} contributors. Below that it is listed, unranked, with the reason.
  • Trends show the monthly average over the last {{ R.TREND_MONTHS }} months.
  • Rankings reflect only the photos people chose to share. They are not a survey: a place with few photos, or photos of only its worst spots, is not fully represented.

Community Notes

Notes add context to a post: a correction, the date of a repair, a source. Contributors rate notes as helpful, somewhat helpful or not helpful. A note's status does not depend on a simple vote count: it depends on agreement between people who usually rate differently.

The method follows the open-source approach of X's Community Notes: every rating is modelled as an overall level, plus the rater's and the note's general tendencies, plus a match between the rater's and the note's position on one perspective axis learned from all ratings. A note's helpfulness across perspectives is what remains once that perspective match is taken out.

{{ N.STATUS_LABELS.pending }}
Fewer than {{ N.MIN_RATINGS }} ratings.
{{ N.STATUS_LABELS.helpful }}
Helpfulness across perspectives of {{ "%.2f"|format(N.HELPFUL_INTERCEPT) }} or more, a perspective lean under {{ "%.2f"|format(N.FACTOR_LIMIT) }}, and at least {{ N.MIN_HELPFUL_PER_SIDE }} “helpful” ratings from each side of the perspective axis.
{{ N.STATUS_LABELS.not_helpful }}
Rated not helpful across perspectives.
{{ N.STATUS_LABELS.disputed }}
Ratings split along contributors' usual lines: one side found it helpful, the other did not.
{{ N.STATUS_LABELS.insufficient }}
Enough ratings, but no agreement across perspectives yet.

Statuses are recalculated whenever ratings change, and every change is kept in the note's history. Editing a note clears its ratings, because they were for the old text. Notes never change a photo's score.

Links to official project records

A post is linked to a project in Infrastructure Transparency only when all of these hold:

  1. the reference number the contributor read on site (a tender reference, a PMGSY package number or a project code from a citizen information board) matches the official record, ignoring spaces and punctuation;
  2. the official record's state includes the post's state;
  3. the kind of work fits the post's category.

A similar name or a nearby place is never enough. Such records may be listed as might be related, clearly unlinked. Every link shows the evidence it rests on.

Costs, agencies and contractors shown with a link are the official record's, with their source. They are for the whole project or contract, not for the stretch or building in the photo, and they say nothing about its condition.
